What to look for in a monitor for coding

Check these six specs before you buy.

Pixel density
Higher PPI means sharper text. A 27-inch 4K has about 163 PPI and a 32-inch 4K about 140. A 27-inch 1440p and a 34-inch ultrawide both sit near 110.
Scaling
Set a 27-inch 4K to 150% on Windows or "looks like 2560 x 1440" on a Mac. macOS has no subpixel font smoothing, so text on a 27-inch 1440p looks soft.
Panel
IPS with a matte coating. VA can smear dark themes when you scroll. OLED text can show color fringes, and static editor layouts risk burn-in.
USB-C power delivery
65 W covers a MacBook Air and most 13‑inch laptops. A 16‑inch MacBook Pro ships with a 140 W charger and may drain on 90 W under heavy load.
Stand and VESA
Get height and tilt at minimum. Pivot turns the screen to portrait. A 100 x 100 mm VESA mount lets you add an arm later.
Refresh rate
60 Hz is fine for code. 120 Hz makes scrolling smoother. In our Nov 22 search, two of the six 27-inch 4K models listed 120 Hz.

Questions developers ask

27 or 32 inch for coding?

+

27 inches fits most desks and gives about 163 PPI at 4K. A 32-inch 4K has about 140 PPI and shows 20% more lines at 125% scaling. Pick 32 if your desk is at least 30 inches deep.

Is a 4K monitor worth it for programming?

+

Yes, at 27 inches. You get 1.5 times the pixel density of 1440p and keep the same workspace at 150% scaling.

Is a curved monitor good for programming?

+

A curve helps on a 34-inch or wider ultrawide, where the edges sit far from your eyes. At 27 inches, most developers use a flat panel.

How far away should a 27-inch monitor sit?

+

About an arm's length, 20 to 40 inches from your eyes. Put the top of the screen at or slightly below eye level.
