Black Friday Fake Deals: How to Check an Amazon Discount

Most "fake" Black Friday deals on Amazon are real prices measured against a weak reference. Check which label Amazon uses. "Typical price" is the median price paid on Amazon in the last 90 days, so a discount against it is likely real. "List Price" is the suggested retail price. It often sits above what the item usually sells for, so it is weak evidence.

Your AI assistant can read the label for you. Black Friday MCP's check_deal tool takes an Amazon link and returns a one-line verdict.

How to check one listing

Use your assistant or the product page.

1
Paste the amazon.com link into your assistant and ask if it is a real deal. With Black Friday MCP connected, it runs check_deal. You get the price, the label Amazon used and a verdict.
2
Or open the listing on amazon.com. Under the price, look for "Typical price" or "List Price" next to a crossed-out amount.
3
If the label is List Price, compare the price at another store before you buy. If there is no label, Amazon is not claiming a discount.

Fake stores are a separate problem

Fake stores and look-alike sites copy a brand to take your payment or your password. Buy from the real amazon.com. Type the address yourself or use the Amazon app. Be careful with deal links in texts and emails.

Questions shoppers ask

Are Amazon Black Friday deals real?

+

Yes, the prices are real. The percent off is what can mislead, because it depends on which reference price Amazon shows. Prices change often, so check the time next to any price your assistant gives you.

What does "Typical price" mean on Amazon?

+

It is the median price paid on Amazon for the item in the last 90 days. Amazon's Creators API reports it as a WAS_PRICE reference. check_deal calls a discount of 15% or more against it likely real, and a smaller one modest.

What does "List Price" mean on Amazon?

+

It is the suggested retail price, supplied by the manufacturer, supplier or seller. check_deal rates every discount against it as weak evidence, however large. The label alone says little about what the item usually sells for.

Is the "Black Friday Deal" badge a guarantee?

+

No. The badge means Amazon is running a time-limited deal and shows when it ends. It says nothing about earlier prices. Some deals are for Prime members only, and check_deal says when that applies.
